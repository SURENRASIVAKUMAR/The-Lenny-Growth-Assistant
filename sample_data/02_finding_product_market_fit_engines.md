## The Sean Ellis PMF Survey Benchmark
Rahul Vohra popularized the operationalization of the classic Sean Ellis test at Superhuman. Instead of guessing whether you have Product-Market Fit, ask active users:
*"How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?"*
- Very disappointed
- Somewhat disappointed
- Not disappointed (it isn't really that useful)
- N/A – I no longer use it

When over **40%** of respondents answer "Very disappointed", companies generally achieve sustainable organic pull and word-of-mouth momentum.

### The Four Fits Framework (Brian Balfour)
Brian Balfour emphasizes that Product-Market Fit is not an isolated state, but a dynamic equilibrium across four interconnected elements:
1. **Market-Product Fit**: The problem is acute, the category is growing, and your solution delivers minimum 10x improvement on the primary friction point.
2. **Product-Channel Fit**: Products are built to fit channels; channels do not mold to fit products. If your product is virally driven (e.g., Loom, Calendly, Zoom), the product mechanics must directly invite or interact with non-users.
3. **Channel-Model Fit**: Monetization model dictates channels. Low ARPU (<$100/yr) requires zero-marginal-cost acquisition (virality, UGC, organic SEO). High ARPU (>$50,000/yr) supports outbound enterprise sales reps.
4. **Model-Market Fit**: Number of potential customers multiplied by your pricing must realistically clear a path to a $100M+ revenue threshold within a defensible market share percentage.

### Expanding PMF After the Core Beachhead
Once core PMF is achieved in the initial beachhead, growth teams must defend against "expansion drag." Expanding to new customer tiers (e.g. moving from prosumers to enterprise IT) demands deliberate re-validation of the Sean Ellis benchmark specifically for the new demographic.

## The Value Metric as the Heart of Growth
Madhavan Ramanujam teaches that products do not fail because of engineering; they fail because companies design the product first and think about pricing right before launch. Pricing is a function of customer willingness-to-pay (WTP) and value perception.

### Choosing the Right Value Metric
A value metric is what you charge for (e.g. per user, per gigabyte, per transaction, per API call).
A great value metric has three properties:
1. **Directly aligns with customer value**: As the customer gets more ROI from your product, the metric naturally increases.
2. **Predictable and controllable**: Customers must understand why their bill went up and believe they had agency over the cost.
3. **Low friction to start**: Does not punish early adoption or penalize team expansion artificially.

#### Examples of Superb Value Metrics:
- **Stripe**: Percentage of transaction volume. If the customer makes $0, Stripe charges $0.
- **HubSpot**: Number of qualified contacts managed.
- **Datadog**: Number of monitored server nodes and gigabytes ingested.
- **Slack**: Active seats (refunding inactive seats automatically through fair billing policy).

### The Per-Seat Trap
Charging per seat is the default for most SaaS companies, but it creates perverse incentives when tools are collaborative. If adding a teammate increases the bill, managers restrict licenses, curtailing internal word-of-mouth adoption and hampering net revenue retention (NRR). Hybrid pricing (base tier + usage/consumption) consistently achieves superior expansion revenue.
